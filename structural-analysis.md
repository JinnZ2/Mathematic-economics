# Structural Analysis by Sector

## Consolidated from Addendum 1-3: Applying Equations Across Economic Sectors

This document applies the 13 structural equations to specific economic sectors, using a consistent format:

- **Mechanism** — what the structure does
- **Measured properties** — relevant equation values
- **Conventional label** — what it is called
- **Measurement-label gap** — how measured structure differs from label

-----

## 1. Financial System

### 1.1 Commercial Banking

**Mechanism:** Banks pool individual deposits into a collective capital pool, allocate loans through centralized committees, and create money through fractional reserve lending. FDIC insurance socializes deposit risk. Federal Reserve provides liquidity backstop.

**Measured properties:**
- SID = 1.0 (fully dependent on government-created currency, FDIC, Fed backstop)
- MSI contribution: fractional reserve creates ~90% of money supply from collective deposits
- MM = 1/reserve_requirement ≈ 10 (90% collectively originated)
- BSC > 1 for systemically important banks (2008: BSC = 3.5)

**Conventional label:** "Private sector," "free market banking," "capitalism"

**Measurement-label gap:** The structure is collective pooling with centralized allocation and socialized risk — properties conventionally associated with collective systems. The label applies to the profit-extraction layer, not the underlying mechanism.

### 1.2 Stock Market

**Mechanism:** Corporations sell ownership shares to distributed holders. Shareholders collectively own productive assets. Boards make centralized allocation decisions. SEC regulates. Federal Reserve intervenes during instability.

**Measured properties:**
- Collective ownership structure (shareholders)
- Centralized allocation (boards, investment committees)
- Government regulation (SEC)
- BSC > 0 during crises (Fed intervention, QE)
- DI: high within corporate governance (wealth-weighted voting)

**Conventional label:** "Free market capitalism"

**Measurement-label gap:** Corporate ownership is structurally collective (shared ownership, centralized decision-making). The label "private" describes the profit distribution, not the ownership mechanism.

### 1.3 Private Equity and Venture Capital

**Mechanism:** Limited partners pool capital into collective funds. General partners make centralized allocation decisions across portfolio companies. Leverage uses collectively pooled bank deposits. Risk is distributed: private parties capture gains, collective bears losses if systemically significant.

**Measured properties:**
- VE/VL ≈ 0.40 (high extraction from portfolio company labor)
- SID = 1.0 (uses collective banking, legal system, currency)
- RI ≈ 5.0 (workers bear concentrated risk; PE partners have limited liability, diversification)
- Structure: collective ownership (LP pool), centralized allocation (GP decisions)

**Conventional label:** "Pinnacle of free-market capitalism"

**Measurement-label gap:** The mechanism — collective capital pooling, centralized allocation, redistribution from labor to capital — is structurally identical to centralized allocation systems. The distinction is the identity of the allocating agent, not the mechanism.

### 1.4 Derivatives and High-Frequency Trading

**Mechanism:** Trading uses borrowed collective deposits, amplified by leverage (often 30:1+). Clearinghouses provide collective risk management. Government backstops apply during systemic failure. No goods or services produced; value extracted from price fluctuations.

**Measured properties:**
- VE/VL → ∞ (extraction with no labor-value production)
- BSC > 1 during crises (2008 bailouts)
- Uses collectively pooled deposits as primary input

**Conventional label:** "Financial innovation," "free market activity"

**Measurement-label gap:** The mechanism extracts value from collectively pooled resources without producing goods or services. Losses are socialized; gains are privatized.

### 1.5 Insurance

**Mechanism:** Individuals pay into collective pool (premiums). Central allocation committee (insurer) decides payouts. Risk is socialized across pool members. Structure: "from each according to risk assessment, to each according to need."

**Measured properties:**
- Collective pooling and redistribution mechanism
- Centralized allocation (claims adjudication)
- Same structural mechanism regardless of whether insurer is public or private

**Conventional label:** Private insurance = "capitalism"; public insurance = "socialism"

**Measurement-label gap:** The mechanism is identical. The label varies with the identity of the administering entity, not the structure.

### 1.6 Quantitative Easing

**Mechanism:** Federal Reserve creates money, purchases bonds and assets from banks and investors, increases money supply, inflates asset prices. Benefits flow to asset holders (disproportionately wealthy). Costs distributed across all currency holders via inflation.

**Measured properties:**
- MSI: collective money creation
- UFR > 1: wealth flows upward (asset inflation benefits top percentiles)
- BSC > 1: collective intervention exceeds private losses

**Conventional label:** "Monetary policy," "market stabilization"

**Measurement-label gap:** QE is collective money creation redistributed primarily to asset holders — upward redistribution through collective mechanisms.

## 2. Defense and Government

### 2.1 Military Contractors

**Mechanism:** Revenue is 100% government-funded (taxpayer money). Pentagon determines requirements (central planning). Cost-plus contracts guarantee profit. Products are collectively owned (military equipment). No competitive market discipline.

**Measured properties:**
- SID = 1.0 (fully collectively funded)
- MSI = 1.0 (uses government money exclusively)
- BSC = high (guaranteed profits eliminate market risk)
- HHI = high (classified, specialized, limited competition)
- Examples: Lockheed Martin (74% government revenue), Raytheon (90% defense-related)

**Conventional label:** "National security," "patriotic capitalism," "private sector"

**Measurement-label gap:** SID = 1.0 with guaranteed profits and no market discipline. By every structural metric, these are collectively funded enterprises with a private profit-extraction layer.

### 2.2 Infrastructure

**Mechanism:** Roads, bridges, utilities, internet backbone built with collective funding. Collectively owned. Free or below-cost at point of use. Maintained by government employees or regulated monopolies.

**Measured properties:**
- SID = 1.0 for the infrastructure itself
- ISR = 5-20x for businesses using infrastructure (receive far more value than taxes paid)

**Conventional label:** "Essential infrastructure" (not labeled "socialist" despite identical collective-funding mechanism)

**Measurement-label gap:** Structurally identical to other collectively funded services (healthcare, education) that receive different labels.

### 2.3 Legal System, Police, Courts

**Mechanism:** Collectively funded through taxes. Enforces property rights, contracts, and market rules. Without this collective system, market transactions have no enforcement mechanism.

**Measured properties:**
- SID contribution: all businesses depend on collectively funded contract enforcement
- ISR: businesses receive far more value from legal system than they pay in taxes
- Prerequisite for all market activity

**Conventional label:** "Rule of law" (not labeled "socialist")

**Measurement-label gap:** The legal system is a collectively funded service that primarily protects private property — collective funding for private benefit, same mechanism as other collectively funded services.

## 3. Labor and Production

### 3.1 Volunteer Labor

**Mechanism:** Labor provided without monetary compensation for collective benefit. Includes volunteer firefighters, political campaign workers, community organizers, youth coaches.

**Measured properties:**
- VE/VL = 0 (no extraction)
- No market transaction (labor decommodified)
- Collective benefit structure

**Conventional label:** "Civic duty," "American values," "patriotic" — never labeled "socialist" despite being non-market collective labor.

**Measurement-label gap:** By strict market criteria, unpaid collective labor is non-market activity. The label "capitalist" does not apply, yet these activities are celebrated by the same frameworks that use "socialist" as a negative label for other non-market collective activities.

### 3.2 Family Caregiving

**Mechanism:** Unpaid labor (childcare, elder care, household management) distributed by need, not market value. Internal family economics: "from each according to ability, to each according to need."

**Measured properties:**
- No market pricing (labor decommodified)
- Distribution by need (not market value)
- Collective resource sharing within unit
- Massive economic value produced but not measured in GDP

**Conventional label:** "Traditional family values" — never labeled with economic-system terms despite non-market structure.

**Measurement-label gap:** The internal economic structure of families is collective and non-market. This structure is celebrated by frameworks that label identical structures "socialist" in other contexts.

### 3.3 Risk Distribution (Workers vs. Investors)

**Mechanism:** Workers bear concentrated, unprotected risk (job loss, healthcare loss, wage volatility, retirement insecurity). Investors bear diversified, limited, subsidized risk (portfolio diversification, limited liability, bailout probability, tax loss deductions).

**Measured properties:**
- RI ≈ 4.14 (workers bear 4x more risk per capita)
- BSC asymmetry: BSC >> 1 for large capital; BSC ≈ 0 for workers
- Stated assumption (RI < 1: "investors take more risk") is not supported by measurement

**Conventional label:** "Risk-based compensation" (investors deserve more because they "take more risk")

**Measurement-label gap:** The stated justification (RI < 1) is contradicted by measurement (RI ≈ 4.14). The compensation structure assumes the inverse of the measured risk distribution.

## 4. Innovation and Education

### 4.1 Fundamental Research

**Mechanism:** Most fundamental innovations originated from collectively funded research: Internet (DARPA), GPS (military), microchips (government funding), pharmaceuticals (NIH), space technology (NASA). Private sector commercializes proven technology.

**Measured properties:**
- SID for foundational technology = 1.0 (government funded)
- Pattern: collective funding → basic research → private commercialization → private profit
- ISR for tech companies: high (built on collectively funded infrastructure)

**Conventional label:** "Free market innovation," "private sector ingenuity"

**Measurement-label gap:** The innovation pipeline originates in collectively funded research. The label applies to the commercialization layer, not the research origin.

### 4.2 Public Education

**Mechanism:** Tax-funded, free at point of service, collectively owned, government-operated. Produces educated workforce that businesses depend on.

**Measured properties:**
- SID = 1.0 (collectively funded)
- ISR contribution: businesses receive educated workers without paying full training cost
- Same structural mechanism as other collectively funded services

**Conventional label:** "American institution" — same mechanism as universal healthcare but labeled differently.

**Measurement-label gap:** Structurally identical to other collectively funded universal services. Label assignment varies by sector, not by mechanism.

## 5. Ownership Structures

### 5.1 Classification Asymmetry

The same structural mechanism — collective ownership — receives different labels depending on who constitutes the collective:

| Structure | Mechanism | Conventional Label |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------|
| Corporation | Shareholders collectively own | "Capitalism" |
| Mutual fund | Investors collectively own portfolio | "Capitalism" |
| Private equity | Partners collectively own companies | "Capitalism" |
| Worker cooperative | Workers collectively own workplace | "Socialism" |
| Credit union | Members collectively own bank | "Socialism" |
| Public utility | Citizens collectively own infrastructure | "Socialism" |

**Measured properties:** The structural mechanism (collective ownership, centralized allocation) is identical across all rows. The label correlates with the identity of the collective members (capital holders vs. workers/citizens), not with the mechanism.

### 5.2 Worker Cooperatives vs. Corporations

| Equation | Corporation | Worker Cooperative |
|----------|------------|-------------------|
| SID | 0.6 | 0.6 (same infrastructure) |
| DI | High (wealth-weighted) | Low (one person, one vote) |
| RI | > 4.0 (workers bear more) | ≈ 1.0 (risk aligned) |
| VE/VL | > 0.3 (high extraction) | ≈ 0 (no external extraction) |
| UFR | >> 1 (upward flow) | ≈ 1.0 (balanced) |

**Observation:** By Smith's criteria (competition, aligned risk/reward, merit-based), worker cooperatives measure closer to productive capitalism than conventional corporations.

## 6. Definitional Analysis

### 6.1 Observed Labeling Pattern

Across all sectors analyzed, label assignment correlates with two variables:

1. **Direction of resource flow:** Downward redistribution → labeled "socialist." Upward redistribution → labeled "capitalist."
2. **Identity of beneficiary:** Workers/citizens as beneficiary → labeled "socialist." Capital holders as beneficiary → labeled "capitalist."

The structural mechanism (collective pooling, centralized allocation, risk socialization) does not predict the label.

### 6.2 Historical Definition Shift

The SD (Semantic Drift) equation measures this directly:

| Era | "Capitalism" emphasis | Dominant value |
|-----|----------------------|----------------|
| 1950s-1970s | Hard work, labor, production, "Protestant work ethic" | Labor-producing |
| 1980s-1990s | Shareholder value, financialization | Transitional |
| 2000s-present | Passive income, investment returns, "make money work for you" | Ownership-extracting |

SD ≈ 2.2% per year. The term's operational meaning has inverted over ~64 years while the label remained constant.

### 6.3 Aristotelian Logic Test

**Premise 1:** If living off others' labor without contributing work = collective redistribution
**Premise 2:** Dividend-only income = living off others' labor without contributing work
**Conclusion:** Dividend-only income = collective redistribution

**Observed classification:** Dividend-only income is classified as "capitalism."

The classification is not consistent with the stated premises. The same relationship receives different labels depending on the identity of the beneficiary.

## 7. Structural Summary

### Measured Properties Across Sectors

| Sector | SID | BSC | Key Observation |
|--------|-----|-----|-----------------|
| Banking | 1.0 | > 1 (2008) | Collective pooling labeled "private sector" |
| Defense | 1.0 | High | Fully collectively funded labeled "capitalism" |
| Infrastructure | 1.0 | N/A | Collectively funded, labeled "essential" not "socialist" |
| Private equity | 1.0 | Variable | Collective mechanism labeled "free market" |
| Insurance | N/A | N/A | Identical mechanism, different label by administrator |
| Families | N/A | N/A | Non-market collective structure labeled "traditional values" |
| Education | 1.0 | N/A | Collectively funded, labeled "American institution" |

### Consistent Finding

Across all sectors examined, the structural mechanism (collective pooling, centralized allocation, risk socialization) is present. The conventional label ("capitalism" vs. "socialism") does not correlate with structural mechanism but with:

1. The direction of resource flow (upward vs. downward)
2. The identity of the beneficiary (capital holders vs. workers/citizens)

### Measurement-Based Characterization

The system measures as:
- **OSDI ≈ 0.77** — predominantly collectively dependent
- **Smith compliance: 0/8** — no measured criteria met
- **DI: high** — concentrated control over collective infrastructure
- **VE/VL > 0.3** — extraction-dominant

The structurally relevant variable is not the degree of collective dependency (which is high across all sectors) but the distribution of control: DI → ∞ (concentrated) vs. DI → 0 (distributed).
